# 02 · Editorial

## Hypothesis
Architects and designers respond to craft and narrative more than to catalogs.
Leading with stories, generous photography, and confident typography makes Material
Logiq feel like a design partner ("Where fabrication meets imagination"), not a
commodity supplier, without making specs harder to find.

## Primary audience
Architects and interior designers in the inspiration and early design phase.

## Key UX ideas
- **Magazine home:** a cover story (16 Prime Steakhouse), materials as numbered chapters, and the Materialize process as an essay.
- **Typographic product index:** one scannable list (name, material, type, NRC, fire) with inline text filters, sortable columns, and a hover photo preview. No cards.
- **Long-form product pages:** a full-bleed hero, a lede, a sticky "At a glance" fact column with the sample CTA, a palette of large textured swatch plates, then specs and documents.
- **Projects as features:** alternating spreads that credit the specified products.
- **A–Z resource library:** grouped by product, with a letter jump bar and inline document links.
- **Stepped sample request:** Selection → Shipping → Review, one question at a time.

## What to evaluate
1. Does the text index make the 26-product range easier or harder to scan than cards?
2. Do specifiers still find data sheets and CAD quickly on the long-form product page?
3. Does the tone raise perceived quality without slowing down repeat visitors?
