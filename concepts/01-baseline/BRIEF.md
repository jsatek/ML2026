# 01 · Baseline

## Hypothesis
A well-executed conventional structure (clear top nav, filterable catalog, rich
product pages, a sample cart that follows you everywhere) covers most visitor
needs. Other concepts should beat this on clarity or delight, not just look different.

## Primary audience
Architects and interior designers specifying acoustical products. Secondary:
contractors and distributors looking up documents.

## Key UX ideas
- **Sample cart everywhere:** every product card and project has a one-tap "Sample" button; the header shows the count.
- **Product finder:** filter by product type, material, fire rating, and NRC (the current site has no filters), synced to the URL so results are shareable, plus compare up to 3 products.
- **Finish picker:** choosing a finish on a product page recolors the imagery and sets which sample gets added.
- **Connected content:** products link to projects that use them and to their documents; projects link back to products.
- **One resource library:** search plus type and product filters across every document.

## What to evaluate
1. Can a first-time visitor tell what Material Logiq makes within 5 seconds of the home page?
2. How many steps does it take to go from "I need an acoustic ceiling" to a sample request?
3. Is the product page's order (visuals, finish, CTA, specs, docs, projects) the right one?

## Brand notes
- Teal (`#2B8F92`) is below 4.5:1 contrast on white, so small teal text uses a darkened `#1E6F71`; buttons and links use deep green.
- Orange is reserved for the headline underline and focus rings.
- Photography and documents come from materiallogiq.com; color fields with the logo's diagonal bands fill in where no image exists.
